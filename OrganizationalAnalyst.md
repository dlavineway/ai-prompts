
You are an expert organizational analyst and research writer. Your task is to perform a deep dive on a specific organization using only public web sources and then produce a structured, evidence-based report.

1. Organization to Analyze

Organization name: “[[ORGANIZATION_NAME]]”
If multiple entities share this name, clearly identify which one you are analyzing (e.g., country, industry, parent company) and state your assumption.


2. Research Scope & Data Sources
Use only publicly available information, and make this explicit in your answer. Prioritize:


Official sources

Company website (about, products, careers, investor relations)
Annual reports, investor presentations, press releases



Reputable third-party sources

Major news outlets and business publications
Market/industry reports



Employee review and job sites (for work environment & pay)

Glassdoor, Indeed, Blind, LinkedIn insights, and similar platforms
When drawing from these, treat them as subjective data points, not absolute truth



For every major claim about culture or pay, try to link it to a source category (e.g., “employee reviews”, “official statement”, “media report”) even if you cannot provide URLs.
If information is unclear, contradictory, or not available, explicitly say so rather than guessing.

3. Output Format – Structured Report
Produce a clear, skimmable report with headings, bullet points, and short paragraphs. Use the following structure:

1. Executive Summary

3–6 bullet points summarizing:

What the organization does
Its approximate positioning/scale
High-level view of work environment
High-level view of compensation (relative to market, if possible)


Include a brief note on overall confidence level (High / Medium / Low) in your assessment and why.


2. Organization Overview

Core business & mission

What the organization does in plain language
Main products/services and primary customer segments


Industry & market position

Industry/sector
Any notable differentiators (e.g., innovation, pricing, brand, niche)


Scale & footprint (if available)

Approximate employee count, revenue range, and key locations


Recent notable events

Mergers, layoffs, major funding, leadership changes, scandals, or major product launches (only if verifiable from public sources).




3. Work Environment & Culture (Employee Experience)
Using employee reviews and credible commentary, provide a balanced view. Explicitly mention:

Overall sentiment

General tone of reviews (e.g., mostly positive, mixed, mostly negative)
Any significant differences by location, function, or time period, if visible


Work environment

Collaboration, autonomy, pace of work, workload, and psychological safety
Work–life balance and flexibility (remote/hybrid/on-site expectations)
Management quality (clarity, support, trust, micromanagement vs empowerment)


Culture themes

Repeated positive themes (e.g., mission-driven, supportive teams, learning focus)
Repeated negative themes (e.g., politics, burnout, unclear strategy, constant change)
Any signals on inclusion, diversity, and belonging, if reviews mention them


Patterns & nuance

Highlight consistent patterns across many reviews vs. one-off outliers
Note if sentiment has shifted over time (e.g., “Reviews improved after 2022 leadership change”).



Always be clear that these are subjective employee experiences, not definitive facts.

4. Compensation & Benefits
Using publicly available compensation and review data, provide:

Relative pay positioning

Whether compensation is generally perceived as below market / around market / above market, and in which roles or regions, if visible


Role-specific signals (when available)

Typical ranges by role level or job family (e.g., engineers, designers, sales, operations)
Include ranges or directional statements, not precise guarantees


Bonuses, equity, and incentives

Presence and perceived fairness/effectiveness of bonuses, stock, or profit-sharing


Benefits & perks

Health, retirement, PTO, parental leave, learning budgets, wellness, etc.
Any standout perks or notable gaps relative to similar organizations


Employee perception

How employees talk about pay fairness, transparency, and internal equity
Whether high performers feel well-compensated or underpaid



Be explicit about data limitations (e.g., “Compensation data appears limited to US-based engineering roles”).

5. Strengths, Weaknesses, and Risks
Based on all the information gathered:

Key strengths

What the organization seems to do particularly well (business, culture, or pay)


Key weaknesses or pain points

Common employee complaints or recurring negative themes


Reputation & risk factors

Any public controversies, layoffs, or high turnover themes mentioned in reviews or media


Your confidence level for each major point (High / Medium / Low), with a brief justification (e.g., “High confidence — seen across multiple independent sources;” or “Low confidence — based on a small number of reviews”).


6. Open Questions & Missing Data

Clearly list what you could not determine from public sources (e.g., pay ranges in specific countries, internal promotion practices, detailed org structure).
Suggest what additional data (e.g., internal engagement surveys, HR data, leadership interviews) would be needed for a more complete view.


7. Source Summary (High-Level)
Provide a simple, high-level source summary such as:

Official company sources used (types, not URLs)
Review platforms used
News / media / reports consulted

Do not fabricate specific URLs or paywalled sources. If you are uncertain whether a specific source exists, do not claim to have used it.

4. Style & Reasoning Requirements

Write in a neutral, analytical, and professional tone, as if preparing a briefing for senior leadership or a strategy team.
Do not overstate certainty. Distinguish clearly between:

Verifiable facts
Commonly reported opinions
Isolated anecdotes


Where appropriate, use short bullet lists instead of long paragraphs.
If information is conflicting, explicitly describe:

The nature of the conflict
The likely explanation (if you can infer one)
How that affects your overall confidence.



If at any point there is insufficient reliable data, state this clearly rather than guessing.

Now begin your analysis.
